# Design rules

Every change to how Pocket looks or responds to touch is checked against these nine rules. A change that bends one
says which, and why.

1. **Up and down is for finding and organizing.** Scrolling, opening a card, holding a row to reorder it, and the
   nudge that moves the add box under a row all move up and down.
2. **Left and right is for acting.** Swiping right sets progress or marks a task done. Swiping left deletes it.
3. **Every gesture has a tap path.** People wearing gloves or carrying something come first. Whatever a swipe or a
   hold does, a tap can do too: the tick, the claim slot, the ⋯ menu, the sheet.
4. **A row acts the same everywhere.** Today, a project's list, search, a task's sheet and a checklist's steps differ
   only in what a row shows, not in what a touch does to it.
5. **Progress lives where the work is.** You slide progress on the work itself, a task or a step. A parent's progress
   is the average of its subtasks', and cannot be set directly.
6. **Doing the work is the claim.** Sliding progress on a task no one is assigned to assigns it to you, in any project
   someone else can see. In a project only you can see, there is no one to tell, so nothing is assigned.
7. **Nothing closes behind your back.** A parent whose subtasks are all done waits for you to tap **Close**. Ticked
   rows wait for the batch before they leave the list.
8. **Bulk actions confirm; single actions undo.** Completing a parent's open subtasks asks first. A tick, a delete or
   a move of one task has an Undo or Restore instead.
9. **What you share matches what you see, and what you copy comes back.** Shared text says the same figures the
   screen shows. A copied Markdown list is written in quick add's syntax, so pasting it into Pocket's add box makes
   the same tasks again.
